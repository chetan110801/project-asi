- **2026-07-16** · **[new]** [`AP7 · Neurosymbolic / hybrid AI`](20-the-approaches/07_ap7-neurosymbolic-and-hybrid-ai.md) (rev 1) — **the oldest bet, reviving: "join the learner and the reasoner."** For decades AI split into two camps — machines that **learn** (soak up examples, like today's AI) and machines that **reason** (follow exact rules, like logic and maths) — each strong where the other is weak. AP7 (Gary Marcus, Luis Lamb) says they were never rivals but **two halves of one mind**: keep the flexible neural **learner** and bolt on an exact symbolic **reasoner**, so the whole both learns *and* reasons reliably. Why the learner needs help: it is a **curve-fitter** — great near its data, shaky far from it — and (per **Fodor & Pylyshyn, 1988**) it lacks **systematicity**, the free power to recombine known parts into new wholes ("John loves Mary" → "Mary loves John"); a **symbol** (like *x* in `x+y=y+x`) gives you rules that hold in *every* case. Grounded verbatim in the **Marcus–Lamb** *Machine Learning Street Talk* conversation (curve-fitting-vs-operations-over-variables, the **robust-AI** definition, the **four-step program**, McCulloch–Pitts's 1943 net that *was* logic, "connectionist as first-class citizen") + Marcus's *"The Next Decade in AI"* (2020, verbatim). Three legs — the **systematicity critique is 35 years old and still unfixed by scale** · the join **actually works**: **AlphaGeometry** (neural guesses the construction, symbolic engine proves it) hit **25/30** Olympiad geometry ≈ gold medalist, and **AlphaGeometry 2 + AlphaProof** reached **IMO silver** (*Nature* 2025) · the frontier is **quietly de-facto neurosymbolic** (reliable models call calculators, code, theorem-provers, databases — neural proposes, symbolic tool checks). Then an honest **judge-it** with four cracks — **the integration problem** (smooth learner vs jagged rules — no gradient flows through logic; unsolved in general) · **the Bitter Lesson** (symbols may be a crutch scale absorbs — and "neurosymbolic" may be too loose to name a distinct bet) · **wins trapped in tidy checkable domains** (no clean symbolic checker for the messy real world) · **hard to falsify** (a six-way big tent). Live-SOTA'd to 2026-07, every fast number dated. 👉 read it — the general form of the "learning vs built-in structure" fight, whose sharpest special case is AP8.

- **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP3 · Agents & cognitive architectures`](20-the-approaches/03_ap3-agents-and-cognitive-architectures.md) (rev 1) — **the mainstream bet, and the deep home for the *agent* idea: "a mind is a system, not one model."** AP1/AP2 work on the model itself; AP3 says the model — however good — is only a **part.** A real mind is a **system of cooperating parts**, so you reach a general mind by **wrapping a reasoning model in an architecture**: an **act-loop with tools** so it can *do* things and check reality (**ReAct** — *"reasoning traces help the model induce, track, and update action plans … while actions allow it to interface with and gather additional information from external sources"*; letting it act cuts hallucination), a **memory that reflects** on its own mistakes in plain words and so learns *without retraining* (**Reflexion** — reflection stored in an *"episodic memory buffer"*), a **memory→reflection→planning** design (**Generative Agents** — *"store a complete record of the agent's experiences … synthesize those memories … into higher-level reflections, and retrieve them dynamically to plan"*), and a **growing skill library** of reusable code so it improves over time and dodges catastrophic forgetting (**Voyager**). Its root is the old idea of a **cognitive architecture** (SOAR/ACT-R) — now with a working brain inside. Three legs — it is **how every capable AI is already built** (Karpathy's *"employee or an intern"*) · the pieces **provably add abilities the bare model lacks** · the capability is **measurably climbing** (METR's task-time-horizon doubling). Then an honest **judge-it** with four cracks — **errors compound over long jobs** (Karpathy's *"march of nines"*; a 95%-per-step agent is ~36% over 20 steps; 50%-reliability hides this) · **the scaffolding may just get absorbed by the next model** (2026's dissolving orchestrator-critic graphs = the Bitter Lesson pointed at AP3 → *is it a road or an app layer?*) · **it can't out-think the model inside it** (inherits AP2/AP8's limits) · **multi-agent "teams" don't yet beat one good agent** (*"mostly in the realm of a single individual agent"*). Live-SOTA'd to 2026-07 (METR ~1 hr Mar 2025 → several hours reported by early 2026; SWE-bench/GAIA/OSWorld; computer-use agents). 👉 read it — the least speculative bet, and the one whose status as a *separate* approach is most in doubt.

- **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP6 · Brain-based`](20-the-approaches/06_ap6-brain-based.md) (rev 1) — **the most down-to-earth bet: "copy the one thing that works."** Every other approach guesses what intelligence is and builds from scratch; AP6 (Jeff Hawkins) says *why guess?* — we already have one working general mind, the brain, so **reverse-engineer the neocortex and copy it.** The hopeful discovery (**Vernon Mountcastle**): the neocortex is not a bag of tricks but **one algorithm copied ~150,000 times** — the *cortical column* — so vision, language, and maths all run the same recipe (*"there is nothing intrinsically motor about the motor cortex, nor sensory about the sensory cortex"*). So AGI is **one** problem, not a thousand. Hawkins' proposed algorithm: each column stores knowledge as a **model** not facts (the tiny-stapler-in-your-head), built in map-like **reference frames** (the brain's grid/place cells — used even for abstract ideas), learned by **moving and predicting** its next input (*"prediction is how a column tests and updates its model"* — the predictive-brain cousin of AP5), with thousands of models **voting** to one perception (the *Thousand Brains Theory*). Its engineering spec: **four attributes** any real mind needs — continuous learning · learning via movement · many models voting · reference frames — of which today's AI has almost none. Three legs — the brain is the **only existence proof** of general intelligence (~20 watts, learns anything) · the one-algorithm idea has **real evidence** (uniform cortex, rewiring, flexibility) · the bet is now **real code & silicon** (Numenta's open-source **Thousand Brains Project**, Nov 2024 → nonprofit Jan 2025; **neuromorphic** chips Loihi 2 / NorthPole). Then an honest **judge-it** with four cracks — **we don't know the algorithm yet** (still disputed neuroscience) · **which biology to copy is unknown** (*planes don't flap*) · **the brain-ignoring approaches are years ahead** (AP1) · **it may be right but too slow.** Live-SOTA'd to 2026-07. 👉 read it — the bet that starts from the one mind we know exists.

- **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP8 · Program synthesis / ARC`](20-the-approaches/08_ap8-program-synthesis-arc.md) (rev 1) — **the approach that attacks scale head-on: "you are climbing the wrong ladder."** AP1 says *pour in more data + compute → a mind appears*; AP8 (François Chollet) says no — you are only ever growing **stored skill**, and **skill is not intelligence.** Real intelligence = **skill-acquisition efficiency**: handling a problem you've **never seen**, from **very little data.** A big text machine is a *"big interpolative memory"* that **fetches** stored recipes; a mind can **synthesize** a new one on the spot (the *"fetching, not synthesis"* charge; the **Caesar-cipher** fingerprint — works for shift 3/5, fails on 9). Its test is **ARC** — little grid puzzles built to **resist memorising** (each novel; needs only a child's **core knowledge**), the measuring-stick from Chollet's 2019 *"On the Measure of Intelligence."* Its proposed engine: **deep learning + program synthesis** — merge System 1 (fast curve-fitting intuition) with System 2 (slow, deliberate **program search**), the intuition *guiding* the search to beat combinatorial explosion. Three legs — ARC **survived the scaling wave for years** · the methods that *do* score on ARC are **program search + test-time training** (not scale) · *better data can't fix a worse architecture* (intelligence is mostly genetic). Then an honest **judge-it** with four cracks — **search explodes without priors nobody can build** · **o3 beat ARC-AGI-1** (Dec 2024, 87.5%, ~$3.5k/task — part search, part expensive scale; Chollet's own "80% in a year" doubt missed) · **each ARC version falls, a harder one restores the gap** (ARC-AGI-2 ~37.6%, ARC-AGI-3 <1%) · **even a solved ARC isn't AGI** (Chollet agrees). Live-SOTA'd to 2026-07, every fast number dated. 👉 read it — the sharpest single idea in the field: *skill ≠ intelligence.*

- **2026-07-14** · **[new]** [`AP5 · World models / JEPA`](20-the-approaches/05_ap5-world-models-jepa.md) (rev 1) — **the fifth approach: the "learn how the world works, then plan" bet.** AP1–AP4 all start from *human text*; AP5 says **text is the wrong food** — a machine that only reads words never really understands the world, because most of what there is to know is *watched, not written*. So: **let the machine watch the world (mostly video), build an inner *world model* of how it changes, and *plan* against it** — imagine "if I do this, what happens next?" before acting (the one thing text machines can't do). Grounded verbatim in Yann LeCun (Lex Fridman 2024): the **four missing pieces** ("understand the physical world … remember … reason … plan"); the **sensory-bandwidth argument** (a 4-year-old's eyes take in ~50× more than all internet text); **autoregressive drift** ("the probability that you stay within the set of correct answer … decreases exponentially"); the **world-model definition** (state + action → next state) and **planning by optimization** (rockets since the '60s); and the trick at its heart — **JEPA: predict the *abstract summary*, not the pixels** ("only … an abstract representation," the "windy leaves" that get thrown away as noise). Three legs — **animals & babies build world models with no language** · early systems already work (Ha & Schmidhuber's "dream," DreamerV3's Minecraft diamonds, V-JEPA 2's robot planning) · the 2025–26 **"world-models race."** Then an honest **judge-it** with four cracks — **unproven at scale** · **maybe text already contains the world model** (the AP1 fight) · **hierarchical planning is unsolved** ("nobody knows how to do this in AI") · **"world model" now names two *opposite* bets** (generative pixel-drawers vs LeCun's non-generative JEPA). Live-SOTA'd to 2026-07 (V-JEPA 2 · DreamerV3 in *Nature* · LeCun's AMI Labs $1.03B · Genie 3 · World Labs/Marble), each dated. 👉 read it — the strongest *architectural* dissent from scale, and AP4's partner.

- **2026-07-14** · **[new]** [`AP4 · RL from interaction`](20-the-approaches/04_ap4-rl-from-interaction.md) (rev 1) — **the fourth approach: the "reward is enough" bet.** AP1/AP2/AP3 all learn from *us* (human text); AP4 says **stop copying humans — put a machine in a world, give it one goal as a *reward*, and let it learn by trial and error, like an animal.** It is the **deep home for reinforcement learning**: the full frame AP2 only tasted — *agent · environment · state · action · reward · policy · the long-run **return** · value · explore-vs-exploit · credit assignment* (grounded verbatim in Sutton & Barto).
