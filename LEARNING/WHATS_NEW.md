- **2026-07-17** · **[new]** [`AP5 · Deep dive #1: learning without a teacher — how a JEPA is really trained (and why it doesn't cheat)`](50-deep-dives/05_ap5-deep-dive-learning-without-a-teacher.md) (rev 1) — **the first deep dive off the [AP5 card](20-the-approaches/05_ap5-world-models-jepa.md), and the one that opens the machine hiding under the card's clean slogan.** The card gave the JEPA trick — "don't predict the pixels, predict an abstract summary" — but never showed **how you train an encoder to make a good summary with no teacher**, or the trapdoor that very trick opens. This page is that machine, in five parts. **(1) The teacher problem — self-supervised learning:** nobody can label a million hours of video, so the data teaches itself — **hide a part, predict it from the rest** (the "right answer" is the hidden part, free). It is the *same idea* as the [next-word](10-how-ai-works-today/01_guessing-the-next-word.md) machine — but a JEPA predicts in the **summary space**, not the raw pixels, and that one choice is the whole story. **(2) The trap the slogan hid — collapse:** if you reward the machine for making its own summaries predictable, it finds a horrible cheat — make *every* summary identical, so prediction is always perfect and the summary means **nothing** (*"A trivial solution is obtained when the encoder outputs constant vectors"* — VICReg). Predicting raw pixels was *safe* from this; predicting a summary you also control is not — **beating collapse IS the JEPA problem.** **(3) The three guards:** almost every self-supervised system is one of three ways to stop the blank — **contrastive** (push **negatives** apart — SimCLR), **regularized** (a variance rule that forces the summaries to stay spread out — VICReg, no negatives), or **distillation** (chase a slow **EMA** copy of yourself behind a **stop-gradient** — BYOL/DINO; the trick **real JEPAs use**, plus masking). **(4) The lineage — from one image to a robot that plans:** **I-JEPA** (mask blocks of one image, predict their summaries — Assran et al. 2023, *"a non-generative approach … from a single context block, predict the representations of various target blocks"*) → **V-JEPA** (video, 2024) → **V-JEPA 2** (Meta 2025, **>1M hours** of internet video) → **V-JEPA 2-AC**, which post-trains on **<62 hours** of robot video to add *actions*, becoming a **latent** world model a real Franka arm **plans** inside — zero-shot, in labs it never trained in, with **no reward** (the card's bet standing up; [AP4](20-the-approaches/04_ap4-rl-from-interaction.md)'s model-based branch realised in a learned latent space). **(5) The blueprint:** it is all **energy-based** (score how well two things fit; collapse = giving everything a low "badness" score), and the JEPA is one **module** in LeCun's *objective-driven AI* — where the proposed answer to the card's Stuck #3 (**hierarchical planning**) is a **stack of JEPAs (H-JEPA)**, still an open proposal. Strict **zero-repetition** (§4.2): the card's *bet / world-model definition / planning-by-imagining / the JEPA slogan / windy-leaves / animals-and-babies / the four bet-cracks* are **referenced, never re-taught**; AP4's *model-based RL / MuZero* is **pointed at, not retold.** Grounded in **written, quotable** sources (the I-JEPA, V-JEPA 2 and VICReg papers — each quote web-verified; a corpus survey of contrastive SSL), live-SOTA'd (2026-07, every fast fact dated; V-JEPA 2 benchmarks + LeJEPA 2025). Four **engine** cracks (distinct from the card's *bet*-cracks): **collapse is beaten by recipe not understanding** (the guards work; the theory of *why* is still argued) · **a non-collapsed summary is not deep understanding** (JEPAs win *perception* benchmarks, not long-horizon reasoning — sharpens the card's Stuck #1) · **you don't choose what the abstraction throws away** (predictable ≠ important — the rare child-in-the-road event is exactly what "drop the unpredictable" discards) · **latent world models are hard to even score** (no pixels to check against). Big question: *the engine solved the entry problem — learning from unlabelled reality at all — but the solved part is perception, while the unsolved part is the deep understanding and long-horizon planning the bet actually needs.* 👉 read it for the *machinery* beneath "predict the summary" — and to see the passive video-watcher become a robot that plans.

- **2026-07-17** · **[new]** [`AP4 · Deep dive #1: the engine — how trial and error becomes a better policy`](50-deep-dives/04_ap4-deep-dive-the-engine.md) (rev 1) — **the first deep dive off the [AP4 card](20-the-approaches/04_ap4-rl-from-interaction.md), and the one that opens the box the card left shut.** The card said RL "improves a policy through trial and error" and even that "learning good value estimates is most of what makes an agent smart" — then never showed **how**. This page is that **engine**, in four stacked parts. **(1) The heart — temporal-difference learning:** don't wait until the game ends; after *every step* nudge your value-guess toward *reward + next-step guess* — the textbook's "**it bootstraps and makes a guess from a guess**," where real reward "injects reality" one step at a time. The gap between the two guesses is the **TD error = surprise**, and it quietly solves the card's **credit-assignment** problem by seeping value *backward* through time. Then the field's most beautiful fact: real **dopamine** neurons fire *exactly* this signal (burst at unexpected reward, silent at predicted, dip at withheld) — Schultz/Dayan/Montague 1997, deepened not overturned by Dabney et al. (*Nature* 2020, distributional coding) — a genuine point *for* the card's "reward is enough" bet. **(2) Two families:** **value-based** (learn each action's worth **Q**, act greedily — the card's DQN/Atari; off-policy, sample-thrifty, but stuck on huge/continuous actions) vs **policy-based** (adjust how you act directly — REINFORCE; fluent in continuous actions like robot control, but noisy and on-policy). **(3) The winner — actor-critic:** an **actor** acts, a TD-**critic** judges by surprise, taming the actor's noise; **PPO** is the workhorse (the card's OpenAI Five — *and* ChatGPT). **(4) The escape hatch — model-based RL:** everything above is **model-free** (a reflex from real tries — *why* RL is so sample-hungry, the card's Stuck #2 explained from inside); the escape is to **learn a world-model and plan inside it** (**MuZero** learns its own model and plans *without being given the rules*, Schrittwieser et al., *Nature* 2020) — which is the whole of [AP5 · world models](20-the-approaches/05_ap5-world-models-jepa.md), so it's handed off, not re-taught. **And the bridge:** this exact actor-critic engine, with the "agent" a language model and the reward a **learned reward model** (from human-preference pairs), *is* **RLHF/PPO** — and swapping the learned reward for a **checkable** one with **GRPO** (drops the critic, scores a group vs its average) gives 2025's reasoning models. So the card's "RL came back" is *this engine*, bolted onto a scaled model. Strict **zero-repetition** (§4.2): the card's loop/return/policy/value/four-cracks are **referenced, never re-taught**; AP5's world-models and AP2/alignment's RLHF story are **pointed at, not retold.** Grounded in **written, quotable** sources (*Grokking Deep RL* — the "guess from a guess," "injects reality," value/policy/actor-critic taxonomy, deadly-triad quotes, all verbatim; O'Reilly et al. for the dopamine tie; MuZero + PPO papers), live-SOTA'd (2026-07, every fast fact dated). Four **engine** cracks (distinct from the card's *bet*-cracks): **the deadly triad** (deep nets + bootstrapping + off-policy can diverge — the clean theory doesn't transfer) · **sample-inefficiency's structural root** (model-based and reward-models each *relocate* the cost) · **the dopamine match is real but not proof** · and the sharp one — **a better engine doesn't touch the bet's cracks and can sharpen reward hacking** (it optimizes a flawed reward *harder*). Big question: *the engine is the most solved part of AP4; the reward and the world are the unsolved part.* 👉 read it for the *machinery* beneath "trial and error" — and to see the game-playing engine turn out to be what trains the frontier.

- **2026-07-16** · **[new]** [`AP10 · Embodiment & sensorimotor grounding`](20-the-approaches/10_ap10-embodiment.md) (rev 1) — **the "a mind needs a body" bet.** Every approach so far tried to build a mind out of information alone — text, images, video, reward. - **2026-07-16** · **[new]** [`AP7 · Neurosymbolic / hybrid AI`](20-the-approaches/07_ap7-neurosymbolic-and-hybrid-ai.md) (rev 1) — **the oldest bet, reviving: "join the learner and the reasoner."** For decades AI split into two camps — machines that **learn** (soak up examples, like today's AI) and machines that **reason** (follow exact rules, like logic and maths) — each strong where the other is weak. AP7 (Gary Marcus, Luis Lamb) says they were never rivals but **two halves of one mind**: keep the flexible neural **learner** and bolt on an exact symbolic **reasoner**, so the whole both learns *and* reasons reliably. Why the learner needs help: it is a **curve-fitter** — great near its data, shaky far from it — and (per **Fodor & Pylyshyn, 1988**) it lacks **systematicity**, the free power to recombine known parts into new wholes ("John loves Mary" → "Mary loves John"); a **symbol** (like *x* in `x+y=y+x`) gives you rules that hold in *every* case. Grounded verbatim in the **Marcus–Lamb** *Machine Learning Street Talk* conversation (curve-fitting-vs-operations-over-variables, the **robust-AI** definition, the **four-step program**, McCulloch–Pitts's 1943 net that *was* logic, "connectionist as first-class citizen") + Marcus's *"The Next Decade in AI"* (2020, verbatim). - **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP3 · Agents & cognitive architectures`](20-the-approaches/03_ap3-agents-and-cognitive-architectures.md) (rev 1) — **the mainstream bet, and the deep home for the *agent* idea: "a mind is a system, not one model."** AP1/AP2 work on the model itself; AP3 says the model — however good — is only a **part.** A real mind is a **system of cooperating parts**, so you reach a general mind by **wrapping a reasoning model in an architecture**: an **act-loop with tools** so it can *do* things and check reality (**ReAct** — *"reasoning traces help the model induce, track, and update action plans … while actions allow it to interface with and gather additional information from external sources"*; letting it act cuts hallucination), a **memory that reflects** on its own mistakes in plain words and so learns *without retraining* (**Reflexion** — reflection stored in an *"episodic memory buffer"*), a **memory→reflection→planning** design (**Generative Agents** — *"store a complete record of the agent's experiences … synthesize those memories … into higher-level reflections, and retrieve them dynamically to plan"*), and a **growing skill library** of reusable code so it improves over time and dodges catastrophic forgetting (**Voyager**). - **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP6 · Brain-based`](20-the-approaches/06_ap6-brain-based.md) (rev 1) — **the most down-to-earth bet: "copy the one thing that works."** Every other approach guesses what intelligence is and builds from scratch; AP6 (Jeff Hawkins) says *why guess?* — we already have one working general mind, the brain, so **reverse-engineer the neocortex and copy it.** The hopeful discovery (**Vernon Mountcastle**): the neocortex is not a bag of tricks but **one algorithm copied ~150,000 times** — the *cortical column* — so vision, language, and maths all run the same recipe (*"there is nothing intrinsically motor about the motor cortex, nor sensory about the sensory cortex"*). So AGI is **one** problem, not a thousand. Hawkins' proposed algorithm: each column stores knowledge as a **model** not facts (the tiny-stapler-in-your-head), built in map-like **reference frames** (the brain's grid/place cells — used even for abstract ideas), learned by **moving and predicting** its next input (*"prediction is how a column tests and updates its model"* — the predictive-brain cousin of AP5), with thousands of models **voting** to one perception (the *Thousand Brains Theory*). Its engineering spec: **four attributes** any real mind needs — continuous learning · learning via movement · many models voting · reference frames — of which today's AI has almost none. Three legs — the brain is the **only existence proof** of general intelligence (~20 watts, learns anything) · the one-algorithm idea has **real evidence** (uniform cortex, rewiring, flexibility) · the bet is now **real code & silicon** (Numenta's open-source **Thousand Brains Project**, Nov 2024 → nonprofit Jan 2025; **neuromorphic** chips Loihi 2 / NorthPole). - **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP8 · Program synthesis / ARC`](20-the-approaches/08_ap8-program-synthesis-arc.md) (rev 1) — **the approach that attacks scale head-on: "you are climbing the wrong ladder."** AP1 says *pour in more data + compute → a mind appears*; AP8 (François Chollet) says no — you are only ever growing **stored skill**, and **skill is not intelligence.** Real intelligence = **skill-acquisition efficiency**: handling a problem you've **never seen**, from **very little data.** A big text machine is a *"big interpolative memory"* that **fetches** stored recipes; a mind can **synthesize** a new one on the spot (the *"fetching, not synthesis"* charge; the **Caesar-cipher** fingerprint — works for shift 3/5, fails on 9).
