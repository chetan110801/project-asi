- **2026-07-16** · **[new]** [`AP11 · Whole-brain emulation`](20-the-approaches/11_ap11-whole-brain-emulation.md) (rev 1) — **⭐ the last card — with it, all eleven bets on the map are written. The "copy it without understanding it" bet.** Every other approach tries to work out *what intelligence is* and build it. AP11 gives up on understanding entirely: point at the one machine already generally intelligent — a human brain — **scan it in full detail (every cell, every connection) and copy that exact structure into a computer.** Run the copy and a mind wakes up — *even though no one understands how the brain makes a mind.* You don't build intelligence; you photocopy it. It is the **opposite twin of [AP6](20-the-approaches/06_ap6-brain-based.md):** AP6 is the *watchmaker* (understand the brain's algorithm, rebuild it, drop the biology); AP11 is the *photocopier* (copy everything, understand nothing) — the purest test on the map of whether *understanding is even needed* to build a mind. Grounded in **Sandberg & Bostrom's *"Whole Brain Emulation: A Roadmap"*** (2008, FHI Oxford — the definition verbatim: *"take a particular brain, scan its structure in detail, and construct a software model of it that is so faithful … it will behave in essentially the same way as the original brain"*; plus its central thesis that WBE, unlike other routes, needs **no theory of intelligence**, and its one big **scale-separation** assumption — that there's a level of detail that is "enough"). The plan is three steps: **scan → translate → simulate.** Live-SOTA'd (July 2026): the **connectome** ladder is real and accelerating — the worm *C. elegans* (302 neurons, mapped **1986**), the **FlyWire** fruit-fly brain (~140k neurons, >50M synapses, *Nature* **Oct 2024**), specks of **mouse** (MICrONS — 84k neurons, >500M synapses + function, **Apr 2025**) and **human** brain (Harvard–Google cubic-mm, 16k neurons, 150M synapses), with **expansion microscopy** and AI tracing speeding it up. - **2026-07-16** · **[new]** [`AP9 · Open-endedness & evolution`](20-the-approaches/09_ap9-open-endedness.md) (rev 1) — **the "grow a mind, don't design it" bet.** Every approach so far tries to **design** a mind — pick a goal, build a system, push it toward the goal. AP9 (Kenneth Stanley, Jeff Clune, Tim Rocktäschel) says aiming is the mistake: the one process that certainly built a general mind — **evolution** — had **no goal**; it just kept producing new, interesting living things forever, and intelligence fell out. So don't build the mind; build a **process that never stops inventing** (an **open-ended** process) and let a mind grow out of it. Its root is the **objective paradox** (from Stanley & Lehman's *Why Greatness Cannot Be Planned*): aiming at a far, strange goal can *block* you, because the **stepping stones** to a great thing don't look like the thing (vacuum tubes → computers), so a goal-compass steers you right past them — *"objectives themselves block discovery."* The fix is **novelty search** (reward *new/different*, not *good-at-the-goal* — it beats goal-chasing on deceptive mazes) and **Picbreeder** (aimless, interestingness-following image-breeding reaches a skull nobody aimed at). Then the modern research program: **open-endedness = producing artifacts that are forever "both novel and learnable"** (Rocktäschel/DeepMind — not a noisy TV, not a finished coin set); the tools **quality-diversity / MAP-Elites**, **POET** (generate the *problems* too — an automatic curriculum, answering AP4's "who writes the reward?"), and **AI-generating algorithms** (design the *process* that generates intelligence — Clune). Three legs — the **only thing that ever built a mind is an open-ended process** (evolution, then culture) · the **objective paradox is real and demonstrated** (novelty search beats goal-search on deceptive problems) · it is **producing real current wins, made far more powerful by foundation models** (DeepMind's *"Open-Endedness is Essential for Artificial Superhuman Intelligence"* 2024; **OMNI** gives "interesting" a usable measure; **AlphaEvolve** beat Strassen's 1969 matrix-mult record; the **Darwin Gödel Machine** self-improved its own code 20%→50%, 2025). Then an honest **judge-it** with four cracks — **you can't define or measure the thing it optimises** (novelty/interestingness/open-endedness all depend on who's watching) · **road or a loop bolted onto scale?** (its wins run *on* foundation models — the Bitter-Lesson question) · **enormously expensive and prone to stall** (nature took four billion years) · **the least controllable path** (a goal-refusing, self-rewriting process is what alignment fears most). - **2026-07-16** · **[new]** [`AP10 · Embodiment & sensorimotor grounding`](20-the-approaches/10_ap10-embodiment.md) (rev 1) — **the "a mind needs a body" bet.** Every approach so far tried to build a mind out of information alone — text, images, video, reward. AP10 says that is the deep mistake: real understanding is not information you are *told*, it is something you *earn* by **acting** on the physical world and sensing what changes. You know "heavy" because your arm has strained; you know "cup" because you have grasped and tipped one. Its root is an old, unanswered puzzle — the **symbol grounding problem** (Stevan Harnad): a word in a language model is defined only by *other words*, like a dictionary written in a language you don't speak, so the chain of words never touches a real thing; meaning has to be **grounded** at the bottom in sensing and acting, and only a body reaches that far. Grounded verbatim in the **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy** *Chinese Room* entry (the **Robot Reply** — *"put a digital computer in a robot body … might do what a child does, learn by seeing and doing"*; *"embodied experience is necessary for the development of semantics"*; Searle's *"just syntactic input"* comeback), **Rodney Brooks**'s *"the world is its own best model"* (behavior-based robotics, 1991), and **Moravec's paradox** (the "easy" things — walking, grasping, seeing — are the *hard* ones for machines, so sensing-and-moving is intelligence's real core). Three legs — the grounding argument is **35 years old and unanswered by scale** · the join is now **real robots**: the **vision-language-action (VLA) / robot foundation model** recipe (take a web-trained model and teach it to output actions as tokens — **RT-2**, *"Vision-Language-Action Models Transfer Web Knowledge to Robotic Control"*; then **π0**, **Gemini Robotics 1.5**, the 2025–26 humanoid wave) · **every mind we know of is embodied** (children learn by doing; ties to AP5 and AP6). - **2026-07-16** · **[new]** [`AP7 · Neurosymbolic / hybrid AI`](20-the-approaches/07_ap7-neurosymbolic-and-hybrid-ai.md) (rev 1) — **the oldest bet, reviving: "join the learner and the reasoner."** For decades AI split into two camps — machines that **learn** (soak up examples, like today's AI) and machines that **reason** (follow exact rules, like logic and maths) — each strong where the other is weak. AP7 (Gary Marcus, Luis Lamb) says they were never rivals but **two halves of one mind**: keep the flexible neural **learner** and bolt on an exact symbolic **reasoner**, so the whole both learns *and* reasons reliably. Why the learner needs help: it is a **curve-fitter** — great near its data, shaky far from it — and (per **Fodor & Pylyshyn, 1988**) it lacks **systematicity**, the free power to recombine known parts into new wholes ("John loves Mary" → "Mary loves John"); a **symbol** (like *x* in `x+y=y+x`) gives you rules that hold in *every* case. Grounded verbatim in the **Marcus–Lamb** *Machine Learning Street Talk* conversation (curve-fitting-vs-operations-over-variables, the **robust-AI** definition, the **four-step program**, McCulloch–Pitts's 1943 net that *was* logic, "connectionist as first-class citizen") + Marcus's *"The Next Decade in AI"* (2020, verbatim). - **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP3 · Agents & cognitive architectures`](20-the-approaches/03_ap3-agents-and-cognitive-architectures.md) (rev 1) — **the mainstream bet, and the deep home for the *agent* idea: "a mind is a system, not one model."** AP1/AP2 work on the model itself; AP3 says the model — however good — is only a **part.** A real mind is a **system of cooperating parts**, so you reach a general mind by **wrapping a reasoning model in an architecture**: an **act-loop with tools** so it can *do* things and check reality (**ReAct** — *"reasoning traces help the model induce, track, and update action plans … while actions allow it to interface with and gather additional information from external sources"*; letting it act cuts hallucination), a **memory that reflects** on its own mistakes in plain words and so learns *without retraining* (**Reflexion** — reflection stored in an *"episodic memory buffer"*), a **memory→reflection→planning** design (**Generative Agents** — *"store a complete record of the agent's experiences … synthesize those memories … into higher-level reflections, and retrieve them dynamically to plan"*), and a **growing skill library** of reusable code so it improves over time and dodges catastrophic forgetting (**Voyager**). - **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP6 · Brain-based`](20-the-approaches/06_ap6-brain-based.md) (rev 1) — **the most down-to-earth bet: "copy the one thing that works."** Every other approach guesses what intelligence is and builds from scratch; AP6 (Jeff Hawkins) says *why guess?* — we already have one working general mind, the brain, so **reverse-engineer the neocortex and copy it.** The hopeful discovery (**Vernon Mountcastle**): the neocortex is not a bag of tricks but **one algorithm copied ~150,000 times** — the *cortical column* — so vision, language, and maths all run the same recipe (*"there is nothing intrinsically motor about the motor cortex, nor sensory about the sensory cortex"*). Hawkins' proposed algorithm: each column stores knowledge as a **model** not facts (the tiny-stapler-in-your-head), built in map-like **reference frames** (the brain's grid/place cells — used even for abstract ideas), learned by **moving and predicting** its next input (*"prediction is how a column tests and updates its model"* — the predictive-brain cousin of AP5), with thousands of models **voting** to one perception (the *Thousand Brains Theory*). Three legs — the brain is the **only existence proof** of general intelligence (~20 watts, learns anything) · the one-algorithm idea has **real evidence** (uniform cortex, rewiring, flexibility) · the bet is now **real code & silicon** (Numenta's open-source **Thousand Brains Project**, Nov 2024 → nonprofit Jan 2025; **neuromorphic** chips Loihi 2 / NorthPole). - **2026-07-15** · **[new]** [`AP8 · Program synthesis / ARC`](20-the-approaches/08_ap8-program-synthesis-arc.md) (rev 1) — **the approach that attacks scale head-on: "you are climbing the wrong ladder."** AP1 says *pour in more data + compute → a mind appears*; AP8 (François Chollet) says no — you are only ever growing **stored skill**, and **skill is not intelligence.** Real intelligence = **skill-acquisition efficiency**: handling a problem you've **never seen**, from **very little data.** A big text machine is a *"big interpolative memory"* that **fetches** stored recipes; a mind can **synthesize** a new one on the spot (the *"fetching, not synthesis"* charge; the **Caesar-cipher** fingerprint — works for shift 3/5, fails on 9). - **2026-07-14** · **[new]** [`AP5 · World models / JEPA`](20-the-approaches/05_ap5-world-models-jepa.md) (rev 1) — **the fifth approach: the "learn how the world works, then plan" bet.** AP1–AP4 all start from *human text*; AP5 says **text is the wrong food** — a machine that only reads words never really understands the world, because most of what there is to know is *watched, not written*.
