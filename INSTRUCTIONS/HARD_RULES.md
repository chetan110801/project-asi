# HARD RULES — the binding standard for collection & synthesis
**The strict, non-negotiable rules for what we collect and how every module is written.** This is the project's `_DOS_AND_DONTS` (modeled on the learner's "hy" system). It is a **final gate**: no module is "done" until it passes §7. Where this adds detail to [`MASTER_INSTRUCTION.md`](MASTER_INSTRUCTION.md), the constitution still wins; this never contradicts it (and §8 forbids drift).

## §1. SCOPE — what we collect (broadened, learner 2026-06-27)
1.1 Collect a **rich corpus across every crucial science/technology domain that can drastically affect humanity and advance society — prioritizing the path to AGI/ASI.** Examples (non-exhaustive, a living queue — see [`DOMAIN_DISCOVERY_SYSTEM.md`](DOMAIN_DISCOVERY_SYSTEM.md)): brain & neuroscience, cognitive science, AI/ML/DL/RL/LLMs, agents, robotics, math & theory, information & computation, hardware/chips/compute, energy, quantum computing, biology/biotech, materials, physics, complex systems, blockchain/web3/decentralization, economics & data, governance/safety/alignment.
1.2 **Study domains independently, foundations → advanced** (like math/physics/chemistry/biology as separate subjects), each on its own shelf — **then merge concepts across domains where they connect.** (This is already the architecture: domain folders = shelves; [`../LEARNING/00_MAP.md`](../LEARNING/) = the one concept-by-concept spine threading across them. See [`LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md`](LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md).)
1.3 **Collect all media types** that carry rich understanding: textbooks, books, research papers & publications, lecture courses, survey/review papers, blogs/essays by serious practitioners, recorded debates & interviews, podcasts, and video/YouTube (transcripts). Debates/disagreements between experts are high-value ([`RESOURCE_COLLECTION_SYSTEM.md`](RESOURCE_COLLECTION_SYSTEM.md) §4).
1.4 **Take as many sessions as needed.** Collection is a sanctioned, multi-session campaign because every collected source is **destined to be processed** into grounded modules (see §3) — it is *not* the "giant unread library" the anti-hoarding rule warns against (reconciliation: [`RESOURCE_COLLECTION_SYSTEM.md`](RESOURCE_COLLECTION_SYSTEM.md) §1 amended v2.4). Order the *processing* by leverage (understanding-per-hour) × AGI/ASI-relevance.

## §2. THE DURABILITY FILTER — timeless principles, not transient specifics (learner 2026-06-27)
2.1 We learn the **grounded, objective reality that sustains the test of time** — the **reason and objective behind** things — **not** the static, replaceable specifics.
2.2 Worked example (the learner's): a 2012 NLP algorithm later replaced by a 2020 one — **we do NOT memorize either algorithm.** We learn **why** the problem exists, what objective each approach optimizes, and the trade-offs — so we could **invent the next one.** The transient artifact appears only as a brief illustration of the enduring idea, never as the point.
2.3 This is the existing **Durability / slow-aging** criterion ([`RESOURCE_COLLECTION_SYSTEM.md`](RESOURCE_COLLECTION_SYSTEM.md) §3, §5) made a **hard rule.** It also reconciles "collect every domain" with the *"rely-on-to-build" test* ([`LEARNER_STRATEGY.md`](LEARNER_STRATEGY.md) §2–§3): include the **enduring conceptual foundations** of each crucial domain; skip **execution grind and transient specifics.**
2.4 Fast-aging facts (current SOTA, who's ahead, latest model) are included only when decision-relevant, and **tagged as dated snapshots** ([`QUALITY_CONTROL_SYSTEM.md`](QUALITY_CONTROL_SYSTEM.md) §4 staleness).
2.5 **THE AI-PROOF CONTENT TEST — wisdom that outlasts the tools, not what a model can hand you (learner 2026-07-02).** The study material is **AI-proof knowledge and wisdom that sustains the test of time** — the enduring understanding that stays valuable even when AI/agents do all the execution. It is **not** a reference manual of things an AI already produces on demand. **We do NOT write a module to make the learner memorize:** function/API/library/framework usage, algorithm recipes, definitions-to-recite, calculation/derivation procedures, or system-design/architecture templates. **For each such thing we capture only the durable part:** *why* it exists, *what* objective it optimizes, *what* it rules out, its trade-offs, failure modes, and the live debate — the insight the learner needs to **direct and judge** an AI doing it, and to **invent the next one.** **Per-item test:** *"Is this here so the learner understands and can judge it, or so they can reproduce it?"* — keep the first, delegate the second. *(This sharpens §2.1–§2.3 and is the same axis-split as §5.2 and [`LEARNER_STRATEGY.md`](LEARNER_STRATEGY.md) §7b. It does NOT reduce §5 depth: a mechanism is still rendered **exhaustively for insight** — it is just never rendered **as a recipe to memorize.**)*

## §2.6 MANDATORY LIVE SOTA / FRESHNESS PASS — the corpus may be stale (learner 2026-07-14)
2.6.1 **Before writing any module — and for every important concept inside it — do your own live web research** for the current **state of the art / state of practice** on that concept. Take the time; **never rely on the corpus alone.** The corpus grounds the **durable** idea but may be **outdated**, and the AGI frontier moves monthly.
2.6.2 **Corpus = grounding; web = currency.** Use the corpus for the enduring *why / trade-offs / debate* (§2); use live search for *"has this moved? what is the latest result? is the SOTA claim still true?"* Record every fresh finding as a **dated snapshot** — *"SOTA as of YYYY-MM"* — because it will age.
2.6.3 **Surface disagreements.** When the corpus and the current SOTA diverge, **say so in the module** — that gap is often the most instructive part.
2.6.4 **Source quality still governs.** A fast-moving claim from a weak source (random blog / press release) stays `[Contested]` / open — never stated as fact; prefer primary sources (papers, the lab's own writing).
2.6.5 **This does not dilute §2 (durability).** The durable core stays the spine; the fresh snapshot is *added and dated*, never the point (§2.2). It **extends** §2.4 from "include fast-aging facts only when decision-relevant" to "**always check** freshness — and include + date the fast-aging detail when decision-relevant."

## §4. SEGREGATION & FLOW — concept-by-concept, not book-by-book
4.1 Modules are organized **by concept, in a dependency order where each concept builds on the ones before** — never "book 1, then book 2." (This is the "hy" flow and the existing dependency ladder, [`LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md`](LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md).)
4.2 **DRY over EVERY reusable unit of understanding — one home, referenced everywhere else (learner 2026-07-02, strengthened).** The no-repetition rule covers **not just "concepts" but any idea, thought, argument, theory, example, analogy, or explanation.** Each is **developed in full exactly once**, in one canonical home; **everywhere it is needed again, reference/link it — never re-explain it**, within a single file or across all files. A repeated *explanation* is a defect. **Reaffirmed & sharpened (learner 2026-07-14):** explain each thing — **concept, technical detail, term, example, analogy, argument, theory** — **in full the *first* time it is needed** (do it properly and deeply *then*, at first occurrence), and **everywhere it recurs, only reference it.** First occurrence = the complete detailed treatment; every later occurrence = a link (at most a §4.2a one-line **reminder**, which is a *pointer, not a re-explanation*). **Zero redundant explanation of any kind** — no re-defining a term, re-deriving an argument, re-running an example, or re-teaching a technicality a reader already met **in the same substance** (re-explaining it *to add a genuinely new point of view, angle, or context* is **not** repetition — that's new content; see next). (What **is** allowed and encouraged: a **genuinely new viewpoint / point of view / angle worth discussing** — one that says *something not already said* — that *adds*, it doesn't repeat; plus the §4.2a **one-line refresher** at point of use before a link. **The test is SUBSTANCE, not wording:** a new viewpoint = add it and discuss it; the **same substance restated in different vocabulary** = the forbidden repetition. "We already touched this topic" is **not** grounds to drop a real new angle. The line: *new point → add · reminder → fine · re-say in new words → never*.) Log reusable units (concepts + the load-bearing named examples/analogies/theories) in [`../LEARNING/CONCEPT_REGISTRY.md`](../LEARNING/CONCEPT_REGISTRY.md); **before explaining anything, check the registry — if it has a home, link it.**
4.2a **SELF-CONTAINEDNESS — a bare link is not enough (learner 2026-06-27).** When a module *uses* a prerequisite concept, give a **one-line plain refresher of it at the point of use, THEN link** to its home — never a bare `[[link]]` that assumes the reader remembers. *(Feedback that prompted this: the 1310 attention demo read "average" precisely because borrowed ideas — dot product, RNNs — were linked but not briefly re-explained.)* **DRY still holds:** the *full* explanation lives in exactly one home; the refresher is a short reminder (one clause/sentence), not a re-teach. **The test: each module must be readable on its own, without jumping away to follow it.** Reconciliation: this *refines* DRY (house rule #8 / [`LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md`](LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md) §5), it does not break it — refresh-and-link, never restate-in-full.
4.3 Big topics **split into several grounded sub-rungs** via numbering gaps (e.g. "LLMs" → a cluster), each at full depth, rather than one shallow file.
4.4 **NO RIGID STRUCTURE — [`../LEARNING/_TEMPLATE.md`](../LEARNING/_TEMPLATE.md) is a flexible palette, not a fixed form (learner 2026-07-02).** Each file **chooses the structure that makes its own ideas easiest to grasp and most pleasing to read** — the order, headings, and shape are decided **per file**, dynamically, by the content. What is **required (the ingredients, in any arrangement):** the frontmatter; a deck; the *Grounded in / Builds on* anchor; concrete-first development; the depth devices (§5.3); inline glossing throughout (§6); the ⚠️ honesty box where hype/contestation exists; the "how a director uses this / delegate vs own" value; and the Connections + registry links. What is **free:** everything else — section order, headings, which devices lead, how it opens and closes. The template just lists the ingredients and a sensible default; **do not force every file into the same mould.** **Exemplar for voice/glossing/depth-devices** (not for fixed layout): the learner's `hy/ayn_rand/Synthesis/36_How_a_Free_Society_Pays_for_Government.md` — plain-read → principle → what-it-rules-out → why-it-matters; "why this example"; anticipate-the-objection; name-the-mistake; verbatim `> quote` + `*(Source)*` grounding. Ours differ: **multi-source** ("Grounded in" lists several) + confidence tags + the durability/AI-proof filters.
4.5 **VISUAL READING EASE (learner 2026-07-02).** Structure serves comprehension and the eye. **Break big paragraphs into small ones** (a few sentences each). Use short paragraphs, lists, tables, small analogies, staircased `###` sub-heads, and whitespace so a **complex idea is easy to interpret and pleasant to read**. A wall of text is a defect even if every sentence is simple. **Length itself is never trimmed for brevity** (concise is last, §5/§6.3) — a file may be as long as its ideas require; readability comes from *layout + simple sentences + glossing*, not from cutting depth, coverage, examples, or glosses.

## §5. SYNTHESIS DEPTH — EXHAUSTIVE on the conceptual axis (v2.3)
5.1 **Render every enduring idea, mechanism, argument, distinction, key result, example, trade-off, failure mode, and live debate** the sources hold on the topic. **Miss nothing** that bears on understanding-to-direct. (Coverage beats brevity.)
5.2 **Implementation/execution stays delegated** — modules are not coding tutorials and don't grind proofs; render math/mechanism for *complete insight*, leave the code/derivation to "direct the AI." ([`LEARNER_STRATEGY.md`](LEARNER_STRATEGY.md) §7b: *cut implementation depth, never conceptual depth.*) **Apply the §2.5 AI-proof test per item:** render a mechanism/algorithm exhaustively *for insight* (why/trade-offs/failure/debate), never *as a recipe to memorize or reproduce* — that is the AI's job.
5.3 Use the **five depth devices** on every important idea: plain read → deeper principle → *what it rules out* → why it matters; per example, *why this example*; **anticipate the objection** and resolve it; **name the exact misconception**; **show each idea click onto the one before.**
5.4 **DEPTH TIERS — calibrated depth per domain (v2.5, learner-approved session 20).** With the corpus now spanning 31 domains, not every domain is written to the same depth. Each domain gets a **depth budget** set by the *rely-on-to-build* test ([`LEARNER_STRATEGY.md`](LEARNER_STRATEGY.md) §2–§3) × AGI/ASI-relevance (§1.4): **Core** = exhaustive (this §5 in full); **Bridge** = working depth (enough to reason and decide, not every mechanism); **Literacy** = compact conceptual map + "how it feeds AGI" (enduring core + the connection, not textbook reproduction). Assignments + audit: [`../RESOURCES/corpus/_ATLAS.md`](../RESOURCES/corpus/_ATLAS.md) §1–§2; flow: [`PRODUCTION_FLOW.md`](PRODUCTION_FLOW.md) ①. **This refines §5.1, it does not weaken it:** *exhaustive* stays the standard **for Core**; tiers set **how deep**, never **whether** — a domain is "covered" only when it yields grounded modules (see Reconciliations).

## §6. LANGUAGE — two axes that never conflict (the 8-year-old bar + glossing)
6.1 **Axis 1 — reading ease (the 8-year-old bar):** explaining prose reads as if for a bright 8-year-old. **One idea per sentence**; split any two-step sentence; plainest connective words (*uses* not *employs*, *so* not *hence*). Read-aloud test. **Reading ease is sentence-shape only — never soften the idea, the depth, or the content.**
6.2 **Axis 2 — vocabulary richness + MANDATORY GLOSSING (learner 2026-06-27):** keep the real vocabulary — every **medium-to-advanced word, phrase, collocation, idiom, and technical/scientific term** — and **gloss each inline in plain parentheses: the general meaning *and* the in-context meaning, in very simple words**, e.g. `gradient (the slope/steepness of something; here: the direction of steepest change in the error)`. Gloss the **first** time an item appears; a short reminder if it returns much later. The reader is a **non-native learner** — when in doubt, gloss it.
6.3 The two axes coexist: simple **sentence frames** carrying **rich, glossed** terms. The file **grows** as a result — that is wanted (concise is last). If 3+ glosses jam one sentence, **split the sentence** (axis 1); do **not** drop a gloss (axis 2).
6.4 Keep the project voice: concrete-example-first, confidence tags `[Established]/[Likely]/[Contested]/[Speculative]`, the **⚠️ honesty box** where hype is common, and the **"How a director uses this / what you delegate vs own"** section.
6.5 **INCREMENTAL LEARNING — the binding way we teach (learner 2026-07-14; emphatic — follow rigorously, every file, no exceptions).** The reader learns in English as a **third language**, with a **limited vocabulary**, and starts with **zero or minimal prior knowledge**. So the whole body of content must be built as **incremental, cumulative learning**: start from zero, add **one small new step at a time**, and always carry **every prerequisite** forward, so the reader is **never confused and never overwhelmed** and each page feels like the natural next step of a beautiful staircase. This is not a style preference; it is a hard rule checked at the final gate (§7). Concretely:
- **(a) Simplest common English in the explaining prose.** Short sentences, one idea each. Use the **plainest everyday word** that works. **Ban** rare/literary words, idioms, wordplay, clever metaphors that need cultural knowledge, and fancy connectives — a florid or "writerly" flourish is now a **defect** (it was over-writing). *Bad:* "a hope wearing a track record's clothes," "the graph goes to God," "smuggles in," "irredeemably." *Good:* say it straight and plain.
- **(b) A PROGRESSIVE LADDER — build on earlier rungs, don't re-teach them (learner 2026-07-14).** The new-plan files form one **learning ladder**. The **first** rung assumes **zero** prior knowledge. Each **later** rung is allowed to **build on the earlier rungs**: when it uses an idea an earlier rung owns, give a **short, simple refresher** (one or two plain sentences) **and then link** to that rung — never a **bare** link (the reader must not be forced to click away to follow the point), and never a **full re-teach** (that breaks DRY §4.2). So each file is *followable on its own* through the refresher, while the full explanation lives **once**, in its home rung, and the curious reader follows the link for depth. Reference earlier rungs freely wherever they help — that is what makes it feel like a beautiful, progressive staircase. This is exactly §4.2a self-containedness, now applied **inside the new simple ladder**.
- **(c) Gloss even more, not less.** Keep the real technical term (it is what the field uses) but gloss it in the plainest words — **general meaning + in-context meaning** — and **gloss the hard word inside a quote too**. When in doubt, gloss.
- **(d) PREREQUISITES ARRIVE BEFORE THEY ARE NEEDED — never confuse or overwhelm.** Order the ladder so that by the time a rung uses an idea, an **earlier** rung has already taught it. A rung introduces **one** genuinely new step at a time and leans on already-taught rungs for the rest (via the §6.5b refresher-and-link). The felt result the learner asked for: it works like **continual, cumulative learning** — every prerequisite is already in hand, so a new page never leaves the reader lost or swamped. If a rung would need an idea no earlier rung covers, **write (or place) that earlier rung first.**

This **refines** §6.1 (the 8-year-old sentence bar), §6.2 (keep + gloss the vocabulary), and §4.2a (self-containedness); it does **not** weaken §5 depth or coverage — *simple English carries deep ideas*. Per the priority order, "understand deeply" still outranks "concise," and now "simple & readable for THIS reader" is the concrete test of the reading-ease priority.

## §7. THE FINAL GATE — run on EVERY module before it's "done"
*(Adapted from "hy" §11 + [`QUALITY_CONTROL_SYSTEM.md`](QUALITY_CONTROL_SYSTEM.md). Switch hats: write, then re-read as harsh critic + confused beginner.)*

**§7.0 — MANDATORY RECHECK PASS (learner 2026-07-14, hard rule).** No file — a module *or any other produced/updated file* — is "done" on first draft. After writing, do a **dedicated second pass**: re-read the **whole file** with fresh eyes, in two hats — **harsh critic** and **confused beginner** — and verify three things, **fixing everything found** before finalizing: **(1) Consistency** — no internal self-contradiction; agrees with the other files, the concept registry, and every rule here (§8). **(2) Explanation** — every point lands (priority-ladder #2); nothing fuzzy; no bare hard word; no wall of text. **(3) Content quality** — depth (§5), grounding (§3 / F), freshness (§2.6), zero-repetition (§4.2), honesty (§6.4). Only then run the checklist below.
- [ ] **Grounded (F):** every factual claim, number, and quote traces to a corpus line; quotes are **verbatim** (grep them); a paraphrase posing as a quote is a defect.
- [ ] **Complete (B):** diff against the source(s); every enduring idea/argument/distinction/example is **present or cross-linked** — nothing important dropped. (Transient specifics may be compressed; enduring concepts may not.)
- [ ] **Non-repeating (C / DRY, §4.2):** each idea / argument / theory / example *explained* once, in its home — **within this file and across all files**; a different *angle or use* is good, a repeated *explanation* is a defect; cross-link (with at most a §4.2a one-line refresher) whatever another rung owns.
- [ ] **Glossed (G):** word-by-word sweep — **zero bare** medium-to-advanced/technical items; every gloss gives general + context meaning in simple words; never a hard word used to explain a hard word.
- [ ] **Simple (H):** the 8-year-old read-aloud bar passes; two-step sentences split; fancy connectives swapped for plain ones.
- [ ] **Incremental (§6.5):** simplest English for a 3rd-language reader (no idioms/rare words/flourishes); **every** medium-or-hard word glossed (incl. inside quotes); the page **builds on earlier ladder rungs** with a short refresher-**then**-link (never a bare link, never a full re-teach); it adds **one** new step; and **no prerequisite is used before an earlier rung taught it** — the reader is never lost or overwhelmed. If a needed prerequisite has no earlier rung, that rung is written/placed **first**.
- [ ] **Deep (D):** the five depth devices present on the important ideas; nothing merely asserted.
- [ ] **Honest:** confidence tags on contested claims; strongest opposing view stated; fast-aging claims tagged as snapshots; not pretending to more certainty than we have.
- [ ] **Fresh (S / §2.6):** a live web SOTA-check was run for the module's key concepts; every fast-moving claim is **dated** (*"as of YYYY-MM"*) and from a quality source; any corpus-vs-current divergence is surfaced.
- [ ] **Structure:** builds on earlier rungs (links back, doesn't re-lay groundwork); registry + WHATS_NEW + `00_MAP` updated; freshness checklist ([`LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md`](LEARNING_ARCHITECTURE.md) §13) passed.
- [ ] **Rechecked (§7.0):** the dedicated second-pass review was run (harsh-critic + confused-beginner hats); every consistency / explanation / content-quality issue found was fixed.
